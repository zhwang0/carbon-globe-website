A WINDOW INTO THE DATA

Data & provenance.

The globe and explorer show a small, reproducible selection from the original CarbonGlobe dataset.

Values come from the process-based Ecosystem Demography (ED) model in their original physical units. They describe simulated ecosystem responses under supplied environmental conditions. They are reference outputs, rather than direct field observations or predictions from a trained machine learning emulator.

What is selected

  • The globe: 13,546 of the dataset’s 54,152 land cells, sampled at every second row and column of the original 0.5° grid. Color shows aboveground biomass at simulation year 40 for an initial forest age of 10 years. Neighboring cells are not averaged.
  • The explorer: six individual cells near regional anchors in the Amazon Basin, Congo Basin, Pacific Northwest, Central Europe, Southeast Asia, and eastern Australia. These are illustrative locations, not regional averages or in-situ stations.
  • Starting ages: 1, 10, 50, and 100 years are available in the explorer, selected from the dataset’s 15 initial forest ages.

Years, units, and display

Every trajectory contains 41 December records: the initial state at simulation year 0, followed by 40 annual steps. This selects month index 11, following the published benchmark; it does not sum or average the monthly records. The paper describes the historical 1981–2021 period, while the chart labels the stored simulation year indices.

The paper describes the three carbon flux variables as annual targets. Their plotted values preserve the benchmark’s December selection, with the paper’s units; no new annual aggregation is calculated.

The globe’s color scale clips at 0 and 12 kg C m⁻², so values above 12 share the highest color. The downloadable JSON retains the underlying values, including negative model outputs, subject to rounding: three decimal places for map values and six for trajectories. No normalization, smoothing, or fitted curves are applied.

How locations are reconstructed

Included cells in the 360 × 720 spatial mask follow the same row order as the source arrays. Their latitude is reconstructed from north to south at 0.5° cell centers. All 3,373 supplied training latitudes and 852 test latitudes match this reconstruction exactly.

Longitude uses the standard west-to-east global cell-center convention, starting at −179.75°. This is an inferred convention: the supplied files and upstream preprocessing do not include an explicit longitude vector. The full coordinate formulas and selected source indices are recorded in the technical notes.
