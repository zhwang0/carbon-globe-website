A WINDOW INTO THE DATA

Data & provenance.

The globe and explorer show a small, reproducible selection from the original CarbonGlobe dataset.

Values come from the process-based Ecosystem Demography (ED) model in their original physical units. They describe simulated ecosystem responses under supplied environmental conditions. They are reference outputs, rather than direct field observations or predictions from a trained machine learning emulator.

What is selected

  • The globe: aboveground biomass or canopy height at simulation year 40, starting from a forest age of 10 years. Smooth surface uses all 54,152 original cells and 1,401 locally interpolated land gaps. Original dots and 3D columns show the same 13,546 original cells sampled from every second grid row and column.
  • The explorer: six individual cells near regional anchors in the Amazon Basin, Congo Basin, Pacific Northwest, Central Europe, Southeast Asia, and eastern Australia. These are illustrative locations, not regional averages or in-situ stations.
  • Starting ages: 1, 10, 50, and 100 years are available in the explorer, selected from the dataset’s 15 initial forest ages.

Years, units, and display

Every trajectory contains 41 December records: the initial state at simulation year 0, followed by 40 annual steps. This selects month index 11, following the published benchmark; it does not sum or average the monthly records. The paper describes the historical 1981–2021 period, while the chart labels the stored simulation year indices.

The paper describes the three carbon flux variables as annual targets. Their plotted values preserve the benchmark’s December selection, with the paper’s units; no new annual aggregation is calculated.

The globe’s color scales run from 0 to 12 kg C m⁻² for biomass and 0 to 40 m for canopy height; larger values share the highest color. The 3D columns use an exaggerated linear display scale, not tree heights relative to Earth’s radius. Blue and green ocean choices change appearance only.

The original point export retains underlying values, including negative outputs, subject to rounding: three decimal places for map points and six for trajectories. The explorer’s original trajectories remain unchanged; their values are not spatially interpolated, smoothed, or fitted.

A surface with explicit limits

Natural Earth land polygons independently distinguish land and ocean. To fill small gaps on land, the surface export uses four surrounding original cells within 2 angular degrees, weighted by inverse squared distance. Connecting paths must remain on land, and filled cells never become sources for more filling. This limits interpolation to nearby supported gaps; it does not project values across large unsupported regions or oceans.

The 30,731 land cells without supported values remain neutral. All 54,152 original ED cells are preserved, including 586 whose centers lie outside the independent coastline mask. Those coastline mismatches are retained as reference data but cannot seed interpolation.

The separate surface asset stores values at 0.01-unit precision, with at most 0.005 units of quantization error. Its provenance mask distinguishes original values, the 1,401 interpolated cells, unsupported land, and ocean. Smooth rendering is a visual aid; it does not increase the scientific resolution of the simulation.

How locations are reconstructed

Included cells in the 360 × 720 spatial mask follow the same row order as the source arrays. Their latitude is reconstructed from north to south at 0.5° cell centers. All 3,373 supplied training latitudes and 852 test latitudes match this reconstruction exactly.

Longitude uses the standard west-to-east global cell-center convention, starting at −179.75°. This is an inferred convention: the supplied files and upstream preprocessing do not include an explicit longitude vector. The full coordinate formulas and selected source indices are recorded in the technical notes.
