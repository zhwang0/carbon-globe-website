05 The emulator

Now teach a network the simulator.

Every step is a training pair: a year of gridded monthly climate, the same kind of reanalysis fields as ERA5 in WeatherBench 2, plus the current forest in; next year's forest out. CarbonGlobe ships it ML-ready, with splits, metrics and eight baselines.

Why CarbonGlobe

Weather AI had ERA5.
Forests had nothing.

AI weather models such as FourCastNet and GraphCast took off because open, ML-ready benchmarks existed. Forest carbon had nothing comparable. The best process-based model, ED, needs more than 100 inputs from many sensing products and about six weeks of cluster time for one global run.

CarbonGlobe closes that gap. We integrate the inputs, run and calibrate ED globally, and package the result for machine learning: inputs, targets, splits, scenarios and metrics, plus a suite of baseline emulators.

Read the abstract

Forest ecosystems play a critical role in the Earth system as major carbon sinks that are essential for carbon neutralization and climate change mitigation. However, the Earth has undergone significant deforestation and forest degradation, and the remaining forested areas are also facing increasing pressures from socioeconomic factors and climate change, potentially pushing them towards tipping points. Responding to the grand challenge, a theory-based Ecosystem Demography (ED) model has been continuously developed over the past two decades and serves as a key component in major initiatives, including the Global Carbon Budget, NASA Carbon Monitoring System, and US Greenhouse Gas Center. Despite its growing importance in combating climate change and shaping carbon policies, ED's expensive computation significantly limits its ability to estimate carbon dynamics at the global scale with high spatial resolution. Recently, machine learning (ML) models have shown promising potential in approximating theory-based models with interesting success in various domains including weather forecasting, thanks to the open-source benchmark datasets made available. However, there are currently no publicly available ML-ready datasets for global carbon dynamics forecasting in forest ecosystems. The limited data availability hinders the development of corresponding ML emulators. Furthermore, the inputs needed for running ED are highly complex with over a hundred variables from various remote sensing products. To bridge the gap, we develop a new ML-ready benchmark dataset, CarbonGlobe, for carbon dynamics forecasting, featuring that: (1) the data has a global-scale coverage at 0.5° resolution; (2) the temporal range spans 40 years; (3) the inputs integrate extensive multi-source data from different sensing products, with calibrated outputs from ED; (4) the data is formatted in ML-ready forms and split into different evaluation scenarios based on climate conditions, etc.; (5) a set of problem-driven metrics is designed to develop benchmarks using various ML models to best align with the needs of downstream applications.

The dataset

From satellites and reanalysis
to ML-ready tensors.

Three stages, done jointly by carbon scientists and ML researchers, so you can start training on day one.

1Integrate 100+ inputs

Monthly meteorology (incl. 24-hour profiles), surface CO₂, soil properties and disturbance: 136 features per month at 0.5°.

2Simulate with ED

Ecosystem Demography v3.0 Calibrated against LiDAR, flux towers, forest inventories and atmospheric inversions

~812 CPU-days on AMD EPYC nodes, for 15 seed ages at every site.

3Package 7 targets

Height, AGB, soil C, LAI, GPP, NPP and Rh for every site, year and seed age.

X812,280 × 40 × 12 × 136sequences × years × months × drivers
v₀812,280 × 7initial forest state
Y812,280 × 40 × 7annual targets to forecast

Task: given X and v₀, forecast Y step by step for all 40 years.

Scenario 1

Sparse to dense

Train on 1/16 of sites (roughly a 4× coarser grid) and predict the rest, just as an emulator would fill in a finer grid.

Scenario 2

Across climates

Scores split by Köppen–Geiger zone: tropical, arid, temperate, cold and polar. Where does an emulator break?

Scenario 3

Across forest ages

Fifteen seed ages from 1 to 500 years test young, fast-growing stands and old, carbon-dense ones.

Problem-driven metrics

Beyond RMSE.

Averaging errors over 40 years hides what matters downstream. Next to RMSE and MAE, CarbonGlobe scores delta error, the error in year-over-year change, and cumulative error, how far a forecast has drifted by year 40.

Speed

≈1,000× faster than the simulator.

A 40-year global forecast took ED 40.6 days of wall time on 20 CPU cores. Most emulators finish in about an hour on a single RTX A4500.
